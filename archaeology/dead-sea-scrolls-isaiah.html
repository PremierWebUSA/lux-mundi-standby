The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ)

2nd century BC (manuscript)Unknown Jewish scribe· Published 5 November 2026

The only complete biblical book among the Dead Sea Scrolls, copied in the second century BC. It shows that Isaiah, a book Christians read as foretelling Jesus, existed in substantially its known form well before Christianity.

Read the text
About this artifact

The Great Isaiah Scroll, catalogued 1QIsaᵃ, is a parchment manuscript of the whole book of Isaiah in Hebrew. It was among the first scrolls found in Cave 1 at Qumran, near the Dead Sea, in 1947, and it is the most complete biblical scroll from the Qumran caves.

Palaeography and radiocarbon dating place the copy in the second century BC. It is one of about 900 manuscripts recovered from eleven caves between 1947 and 1956. The scroll is displayed in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem, and high-resolution images are published online.

Its importance for the history of Jesus is indirect but real. The Gospels and early Christians drew heavily on Isaiah: the Immanuel sign (7:14), the suffering servant (52:13-53:12) and the anointed herald of 61:1-2. The scroll proves that these texts existed, in substantially the form later known, long before Jesus was born.

It differs from the later Masoretic text in many small details, which matters for any argument resting on the exact wording of a verse, and it does not record how Qumran read these passages. Its witness is to the text itself: the words Christians read as pointing to Jesus were already written centuries before his birth.

The object

The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ) is a long parchment scroll containing the entire book of Isaiah in Hebrew, written in columns on sheets sewn together. It is the most complete biblical scroll from Qumran Cave 1, where it was found in 1947.

Date

The manuscript is dated on palaeographic and radiocarbon grounds to the second century BC. That is the date of this copy; the book of Isaiah itself is older.

What it establishes

Isaiah existed as a book, in substantially the form known from later tradition, centuries before Christianity. The scroll also shows textual variation: it differs from the Masoretic text in many details, which matters for any argument that depends on the exact wording of a quoted verse.

Open questions

The scroll does not tell us whether the Qumran community, or anyone else, read Isaiah as a prediction of Jesus. Whether these passages (7:14; 53; 61) were written about him is a question of interpretation that the scroll cannot settle. What it does settle is the pre-Christian history of the text: the words Christians later read as fulfilled were already there.

The wider find

The scroll is one of about 900 manuscripts from eleven caves near Qumran, recovered between 1947 and 1956 and published progressively.

Where it is now

The scroll is displayed in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem. The Israel Museum and the Israel Antiquities Authority publish high-resolution images.
