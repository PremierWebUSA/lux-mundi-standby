The Third Letter of John

c. AD 90–110’The elder’; traditionally John the apostle· Published 5 November 2026

A private letter from “the elder” to Gaius about hospitality to travelling missionaries who went out “for the sake of the Name”. It does not name Jesus, but it opens a window on the life of the early congregations.

What it says of ChristRead the text
About this witness

3 John is a private letter from “the elder” to a Christian named Gaius, praising his hospitality to travelling missionaries and complaining about a local leader, Diotrephes, who refused to receive the elder’s envoys. It is the shortest book in the New Testament, and tradition places it with 1 and 2 John as the work of John the apostle.

It is usually dated c. AD 90–110. Short and private, it was rarely quoted, and its acceptance was slow: Origen, the Alexandrian scholar of the third century, noted that not all accepted the second and third letters of John, and Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea and historian of the church, writing c. AD 325, listed it among the books disputed by some but known to most. Many scholars think its author was an elder of the Johannine circle distinct from the apostle.

3 John does not mention Jesus by name. Its interest is social: it shows networks of travelling preachers who went out “for the sake of the Name” (verse 7), dependent on local hospitality, and disputes over authority within early congregations.

The text

Complete text of The Third Letter of John in the World English Bible, with verse numbers, in a single chapter. The WEB translators’ footnotes (mostly alternative renderings and manuscript notes) are omitted.

Chapter 1

1 The elder to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth.

2 Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be healthy, even as your soul prospers. 3 For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and testified about your truth, even as you walk in truth. 4 I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth.

5 Beloved, you do a faithful work in whatever you accomplish for those who are brothers and strangers. 6 They have testified about your love before the assembly. You will do well to send them forward on their journey in a way worthy of God, 7 because for the sake of the Name they went out, taking nothing from the Gentiles. 8 We therefore ought to receive such, that we may be fellow workers for the truth.

9 I wrote to the assembly, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, doesn’t accept what we say. 10 Therefore, if I come, I will call attention to his deeds which he does, unjustly accusing us with wicked words. Not content with this, he doesn’t receive the brothers himself, and those who would, he forbids and throws out of the assembly.

11 Beloved, don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good. He who does good is of God. He who does evil hasn’t seen God. 12 Demetrius has the testimony of all, and of the truth itself; yes, we also testify, and you know that our testimony is true.

13 I had many things to write to you, but I am unwilling to write to you with ink and pen; 14 but I hope to see you soon. Then we will speak face to face.

Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.
