The Prophets

The chapters of the law, the psalms and the prophets that the first Christians read as pointing to Jesus, given in full so each promise can be read in its own setting.

13writings17,406words

The law

3 writings
  1. 500 BCFinal form c. 6th–5th century BC

    Genesis: the call of Abraham

    Traditionally Moses

    The first book of the Torah. In chapter 12 God calls Abraham and promises that through him all the families of the earth will be blessed, a promise Paul read as the gospel announced in advance.

     →
  2. 500 BCFinal form c. 6th–5th century BC

    Exodus: the first Passover

    Traditionally Moses

    The second book of the Torah. Chapter 12 institutes the Passover, with its unblemished lamb whose blood marks the doors and whose bones are not to be broken, a pattern the New Testament sees in the death of Jesus.

     →
  3. 500 BCFinal form c. 6th–5th century BC

    Numbers: the bronze serpent and the star

    Traditionally Moses

    The fourth book of the Torah, following Israel through the wilderness. Chapter 21 tells of the bronze serpent lifted on a pole, and chapter 24 of Balaam’s oracle of a star rising out of Jacob.

     →

The kingdom and the psalms

2 writings
  1. 600 BCc. 7th–6th century BC

    2 Samuel: the promise to David

    Anonymous; Jewish tradition names Samuel, Nathan and Gad

    The story of David’s reign, c. 1000 BC. In chapter 7 God promises David, through the prophet Nathan, a son and a throne established forever, the root of Israel’s hope for a messiah from David’s line.

     →
  2. 700 BCc. 1000–400 BC

    Psalms: the songs read of the Messiah

    Traditionally David and other psalmists

    Eight of Israel’s psalms that the New Testament reads as speaking of Jesus: royal songs of the king God enthrones, and laments of a righteous sufferer, including Psalm 22, whose opening words Jesus cried from the cross.

The prophets

8 writings
  1. 700 BCc. 740–500 BC

    Isaiah: the chapters read as prophecy

    Traditionally Isaiah son of Amoz

    The longest of the prophets, from Jerusalem in the eighth century BC. These chapters, from the sign of Immanuel to the suffering servant of chapter 53, are the ones the New Testament draws on most to explain who Jesus was.

     →
  2. 600 BCc. 627–580 BC

    Jeremiah: Rachel’s weeping and the new covenant

    Jeremiah, with his scribe Baruch

    The prophet of Jerusalem’s last years before the Babylonian conquest. Chapter 31 holds Rachel weeping for her children and the promise of a new covenant written on the heart.

     →
  3. 530 BC6th century BC setting; c. 165 BC on the critical view

    Daniel: the vision of the Son of Man

    Traditionally Daniel

    Visions of the empires and of God’s coming kingdom. In chapter 7 one like a son of man comes with the clouds to receive an everlasting dominion, the figure Jesus named himself by.

     →
  4. 740 BCc. 750–725 BC

    Hosea: out of Egypt I called my son

    Hosea son of Beeri

    A prophet of the northern kingdom in the eighth century BC. Chapter 11 remembers God calling Israel, his son, out of Egypt, a line Matthew applies to the return of the child Jesus.

     →
  5. 500 BCDate disputed, c. 9th–4th century BC

    Joel: the Spirit poured out

    Joel son of Pethuel

    A prophet of the day of Yahweh. Chapter 2 promises that God will pour out his Spirit on all flesh, the passage Peter quoted at Pentecost.

     →
  6. 600 BCc. 8th–5th century BC

    Jonah: three days in the fish

    Traditionally Jonah son of Amittai

    The story of a prophet who flees God’s call and is swallowed by a great fish for three days and three nights, the sign Jesus gave of his own death and rising.

     →
  7. 720 BCc. 735–700 BC

    Micah: the ruler from Bethlehem

    Micah of Moresheth

    A prophet of Judah in the late eighth century BC. Chapter 5 looks for a ruler of Israel from Bethlehem, David’s town, whose origins are from of old.

     →
  8. 500 BCc. 520–450 BC

    Zechariah: the humble king and the stricken shepherd

    Traditionally Zechariah son of Berechiah

    A prophet of the return from exile. These chapters, of the king riding on a donkey, the thirty pieces of silver, the one who was pierced and the shepherd struck, shape the Gospels’ account of Jesus’s last days.
