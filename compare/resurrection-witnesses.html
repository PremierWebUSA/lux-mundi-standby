The earliest list of resurrection witnesses

Paul’s list of those to whom the risen Christ appeared is widely regarded as the earliest written testimony to the resurrection. Paul calls it a tradition he received, and many scholars date its formulation to within a few years of the crucifixion, since Paul met Peter and James in Jerusalem around AD 35–37.

Paul’s list and the Gospel narratives overlap only partially. An appearance to Peter alone, which heads Paul’s list, is never narrated in the Gospels: Luke merely mentions it in passing, and Mark’s young man singles Peter out by name. The comparison suggests the narratives and the formula grew from shared but differently remembered traditions.

  • Paul names Cephas (Peter) first; Luke’s disciples report “The Lord is risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon!” without narrating the appearance.
  • Mark’s young man singles out “his disciples and Peter” for the message.
  • Paul’s list has no women, though the Gospels make women the first witnesses of the empty tomb; it includes James and the five hundred, whom the Gospels never mention.
