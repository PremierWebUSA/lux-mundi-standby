Peter’s confession at Caesarea Philippi

Peter’s declaration that Jesus is the Messiah is the hinge of the Synoptic story: from here on Jesus begins to speak of his coming suffering. Comparing the three versions shows clearly how Matthew and Luke edited Mark, each expanding or trimming the same exchange.

Matthew’s additional blessing of Peter, with its sayings about the rock and the keys, is found only in his Gospel. Whether it comes from an independent tradition or from Matthew’s own shaping is debated, and it has been central to later disagreements between churches about Peter’s role and its continuation.

  • Peter’s answer grows: “the Christ” (Mark), “the Christ, the Son of the living God” (Matthew), “the Christ of God” (Luke).
  • Only Matthew has Jesus’s blessing of Peter, the play on Peter and rock, and the keys of the Kingdom.
  • Luke drops the place name and sets the question while Jesus is praying alone, a characteristic Lukan touch.

13 Now when Jesus came into the parts of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying, “Who do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?”

17 Jesus answered him, “Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven. 18 I also tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my assembly, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it. 19 I will give to you the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven, and whatever you bind on earth will have been bound in heaven; and whatever you release on earth will have been released in heaven.” 20 Then he commanded the disciples that they should tell no one that he was Jesus the Christ.
