The Last Supper

The words over the bread and the cup survive in two forms. Mark and Matthew share one wording; Luke’s is close to Paul’s. Paul’s letter to the Corinthians, written in the mid-50s, is probably the earliest written account, and Paul says he is handing on a tradition he himself received.

Comparing them shows a tradition that was already being repeated in worship within about twenty-five years of the crucifixion. The core is stable: bread, cup, body, blood, covenant. The variations in wording are small but meaningful, and they have been studied closely both by historians and by theologians of the Eucharist.

  • Paul and Luke share the command “Do this in memory of me” and the phrase “the new covenant” over the cup; Mark and Matthew have “my blood of the new covenant” without the command.
  • Matthew alone adds “for the remission of sins” to the cup saying.
  • Luke mentions a cup before the bread as well as after supper; some manuscripts omit Luke 22:19b–20, a well-known textual question.
