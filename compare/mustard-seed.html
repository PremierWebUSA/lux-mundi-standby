The mustard seed: Thomas and the Synoptics

The Gospel of Thomas, a collection of sayings known complete only in a Coptic manuscript from Nag Hammadi, shares many sayings with the Synoptic Gospels. Scholars disagree about whether Thomas depends on the canonical Gospels or preserves an independent line of tradition, and the parable of the mustard seed is a frequent test case.

Thomas’ version is brief and, like almost all his sayings, lacks any narrative setting, though here the disciples’ question provides a frame. Each Synoptic evangelist introduces the parable differently, and Matthew and Luke share wording against Mark, which is usually explained by their common use of a second source.

  • Thomas, Mark and Matthew all contrast the tiny seed with the large plant; Luke drops the contrast.
  • Thomas’ seed must fall “on tilled soil”, a detail without Synoptic parallel that some read as a theme of the right disposition.
  • Mark and Thomas speak of a large plant; Matthew and Luke call it a tree, a point often cited for their shared non-Markan source (Q).
