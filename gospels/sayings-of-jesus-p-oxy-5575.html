Sayings of Jesus (Papyrus Oxyrhynchus 5575)

Copy 2nd century (c. 150–200); published 2023Unknown; tr. Mark M. Mattison· Published 5 November 2026

A small second-century papyrus, published in 2023, preserving sayings of Jesus close to Matthew 6, Luke 12 and the Gospel of Thomas: on anxiety, the birds and the lilies, fasting from the world, and seeking the kingdom.

What it says of ChristRead the text
About this witness

Papyrus Oxyrhynchus 5575 is a small leaf of Greek papyrus, written on both sides, that preserves a run of sayings attributed to Jesus. It was published in 2023 in volume 87 of The Oxyrhynchus Papyri, and its editors date the handwriting to the second century. Mark M. Mattison’s translation, with his notes on the parallels, is given here.

The damaged lines speak of a rich man who died, of not worrying about food and clothing, of fasting from the world, of the birds and the grass of the field, and of seeking the kingdom. They closely resemble Matthew 6, Luke 12 and sayings 27, 36 and 63 of the Gospel of Thomas, and seem to combine these strands.

Whether the fragment comes from a known gospel, a lost sayings collection or an anthology drawn from several sources is still an open question, as is its exact relation to the other traditions. What it shows clearly is that sayings of Jesus in forms close to the Synoptic Gospels and Thomas were being copied in Egypt in the second century.

The text

The complete text of P.Oxy. 5575 (recto and verso) in Mark M. Mattison’s translation, followed by his notes on parallel passages. Square brackets mark restored words.

Symbols: [ ] Gap in the text; ( ) Editorial insertion

recto (→)

[…] (the rich man) died. [I tell you, don’t] be anxious about [your life], what you’ll eat, [nor the] body, what [you’ll wear], because I tell you, [unless] you fast from [the world], you’ll never find [the kingdom], and unless you […] the world, [you’ll never …] the Father [… the] birds, how […] and [your] heavenly Father [feeds them …] so you […] much […]

verso (↓)

[…] how [they grow …] Solomon […] in [his] glory […] the Father [clothes] grass which [dries up] and is thrown into the oven, [he’ll clothe] you […] so you […] also […] because [your] Father [knows] your need, [but] look for [the kingdom and all these things will be given …]

Notes on the translation

recto (→)

(the rich man) died. Cp. Thomas 63: “a rich man … died.” See also Luke 12:16-21.

[don’t] be anxious. Cp. Matthew 6:25; Luke 12:22; Thomas 36.

[unless] you fast from [the world]. Cp. Thomas 27.

[the] birds. Cp. Matthew 6:26; Luke 12:24.

verso (↓)

how [they grow]. Cp. Matthew 6:28-30; Luke 12:27,28; Thomas 36.

so you. Cp. Matthew 6:32,33; Luke 12:30,31.
