Herod the Great

Who they were

Herod, an Idumaean appointed king of Judea by the Roman Senate, ruled from 37 to 4 BC. He rebuilt the Jerusalem Temple on a grand scale and founded Caesarea Maritima, but was also remembered for ruthlessness, including the execution of his wife Mariamne and several of his sons.

Matthew and Luke date Jesus’s birth to the end of his reign, which places it about 6–4 BC. Matthew tells of the magi’s visit and of Herod’s order to kill the boys of Bethlehem, an episode no other ancient source records; historians are divided over whether it reflects a memory consistent with his character or a narrative modelled on the story of Moses.
