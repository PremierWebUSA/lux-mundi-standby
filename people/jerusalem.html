About this place

The religious centre of Judaism, dominated in Jesus’s day by the Temple that Herod the Great had rebuilt. The Gospels place Jesus’s final days, trial, crucifixion and burial here, and the earliest community of his followers, led by Peter and later by James, gathered here.

The Romans destroyed the Temple in AD 70 and, after the Bar Kokhba revolt, Hadrian refounded the city as Aelia Capitolina. Finds such as the Caiaphas ossuary come from its tombs, and the Israel Museum keeps many of the objects in this archive.

    The Caiaphas ossuary

    Unknown (Jerusalem family tomb)

    An ornate limestone bone box from a first-century Jerusalem family tomb, inscribed “Joseph son of Caiaphas”. Many scholars hold that it belonged to the family of the high priest Caiaphas who, in the Gospels, questioned Jesus.

  2. AD 621st century AD (claimed); surfaced 2002

    The James ossuary (disputed)

    Unknown

    A limestone bone box inscribed “James, son of Joseph, brother of Jesus”, announced in 2002 without an excavation record. Experts still debate whether the whole inscription is ancient; its owner’s 2012 acquittal left that question to the evidence.

     →
  3. AD 80before AD 62 if by James; otherwise c. AD 70–100

    The Letter of James

    Traditionally James, brother of Jesus; disputed

    A letter of moral teaching from “James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ”, traditionally the brother of Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church. Many of its sayings echo the Sermon on the Mount.
