James the Just

Who they were

James was one of the brothers of Jesus named in Mark 6:3. He is not among the Twelve, and John says Jesus’s brothers did not at first believe in him, but Paul lists James among those to whom the risen Jesus appeared and calls him ‘the Lord’s brother’. By the 40s he led the Jerusalem community. Catholic and Orthodox traditions understand ‘brother’ as a cousin or stepbrother.

Josephus reports that in AD 62 the high priest Ananus had ‘the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James’ stoned, a passage most historians accept as authentic. Scholars still debate whether the ossuary inscribed ‘brother of Jesus’ is his. Hegesippus, quoted by Eusebius, remembered him as ‘the Just’, and the Letter of James bears his name.
