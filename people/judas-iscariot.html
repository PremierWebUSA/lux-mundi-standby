Judas Iscariot

Who they were

Judas Iscariot was one of the Twelve, remembered in every Gospel as the disciple who handed Jesus over to the chief priests. The meaning of ‘Iscariot’ is uncertain: ‘man from Kerioth’ and ‘dagger-man’ are among the proposals. His motive is unclear; Matthew mentions thirty pieces of silver and John calls him a thief, while Luke and John say Satan entered him.

Matthew and Acts give different accounts of his death, and Papias preserved a third, gruesome tradition. In the second century some groups read his role positively: Irenaeus mentions a Gospel of Judas, and a Coptic Gospel of Judas published in 2006, whose portrait of him scholars read in different ways, presents him as the recipient of secret teaching.
