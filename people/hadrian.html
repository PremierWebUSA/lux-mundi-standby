Hadrian

Who they were

Hadrian reigned from 117 to AD 138. About 124–125 he wrote to Minucius Fundanus, proconsul of Asia, that Christians should be tried only on formal charges in court and that malicious accusers should be punished. The rescript survives only through Justin Martyr and Eusebius.

After the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135) he refounded Jerusalem as the Roman colony Aelia Capitolina and barred Jews from the city, ending the line of Jewish-Christian bishops there. The apologist Quadratus addressed his defence of Christianity to him.
